## 2. How the species were chosen

Source: `data/species/shortlist_v1.csv` (381), minus the 25 pilot species, the 35 batch-1 species (the 6 batch-1 reserves are IN batch 2) and the four batch-1 gate failures (neem, tamarind, trumpet tree, Douglas-fir: not retried). Rules, in order:
1. **Exclude**: IUCN other than LC or NE (jaguar, plains zebra, African buffalo, Indian flying fox, European ash, sugi, American bumble bee), narrow or small ranges (Protea repens, Leucadendron salignum, Bougainvillea), species whose records mostly come from introduced or planted populations (house sparrow, starling, mallard and Canada goose as managed or domestic, honey bee, invasive insects, Eucalyptus globulus, Callery pear, Cedrus deodara, Acer palmatum), cannabis, wild-relative crops whose GBIF records are weeds (wild carrot, wild cherry, white clover).
2. **Plants in bulk with WCVP native ranges** (no curation): trees 20, wild plants 11, crops 11 (crops carry no native mask; wording "climate like where X plants are recorded", unless cultivated records are verified: the W2 report states `cultivated_managed_records.kept` per crop and the engine switches the wording only if it is above zero [U that it stays zero, as for batch 1]).
3. **Animals with curated native regions** (our judgement, continent grammar of `ctw.species.native`, deliberately generous because the range check tests commission): birds 23, mammals 12, insects and arachnids 12, reptiles 6, amphibians 4, freshwater fish 5. **Self-checked in a second pass** (section 4).
4. **Migratory and partial-migrant birds use the breeding-season handling of W2 section 11**: 8 species (turkey vulture, red-tailed hawk, great blue heron, American crow, Eurasian blackbird, common chaffinch, white wagtail, common buzzard) get a seasonal cell table (May to July records inside the breeding mask, northern breeders) and only the breeding-only fit is run (output id `<id>_breeding`). The other 15 birds are residents or sedentary and use all-year records. [U] some residents have local movements; the notes are in `curated_native_v2.csv`.
5. **Herps and fish** are included because their two target-group SQL downloads (`herp`: Reptilia and Amphibia; `fish`: Actinopterygii) are requested in the same acquisition round as the species downloads. Group mammal-like grids are NOT used; new grids `s2_tg_density_herp_v1.npz`, `s2_tg_density_fish_v1.npz`. Fish records on water cells are partly lost by the land step; a freshwater fish niche on air climate is a weak proxy [U], the gates decide.
6. **Quotas**: the shortlist quotas (tree 90, bird 90, mammal 40, wild plant 40, crop 40, insect 48, herp 20, fish 11) scaled to about 30 percent of what remains, balanced across the six regions (batch 1 and the pilot are heavy on North America and Europe birds); counts: amphibian 4, bird 23, crop 11, fish 5, insect_arachnid 12, mammal 12, reptile 6, tree 20, wild_plant 11. By shortlist region: AFRICA 11, ASIA 12, EUROPE 31, NORTH_AMERICA 25, OCEANIA 12, SOUTH_AMERICA 13.

Download batches (unfiltered GBIF records, thousands; cap 24 M per download, one DOI per batch as in batch 1 [U whether GBIF accepts a batch DOI in a derived dataset]): b1 29,076, b2 23,998, b3 23,998, b4 23,997, b5 23,998, b6 23,999, b7 19,641. ## 3. Pipeline (batch 1 engine; what changed)

Same engine as batch 1 (`s2run.py` is `s1run.py` with product name `s2`). Changes, all additive: (1) **all downloads up front**: the two SQL target-group downloads and the 7 species batches are requested as soon as a GBIF slot is free (3 at a time) and the free-slot check runs again before each processing step, so no sequential rounds; (2) **empty-table guard**: a batch with any species of 0 cells or 0 records stores nothing and the run fails loudly (batch 1 stored empty tables); assemble fails if any species has no cells, no kept cells after the native mask, or (migrant) no breeding cells, and commits a small `data/species/batch2_w2_summary.csv` (records, cells, kept, removed, ok flag per species) so the check does not need the release (the sandbox cannot download release assets); the fit workflow only fits species with `ok = True`; (3) migrants keep the month of every record during cleaning (`keep_months`) and assemble writes `s2_occ_cells_seasonal_v1.parquet` and `s2_w2_seasonal_report_v1.json` in the layout of the pilot's seasonal product, no second download or re-fetch; (4) `inputs.species_inputs` reads `W3_PRODUCT=s2` (prefix `s2_`, seasonal and `s2_tg_density_*` names); `tg.py` gets `EXTRA_GROUPS` and `S2_GROUP_TO_TG` (the pilot's `GROUPS` is unchanged, so its acquire stage cannot request them); (5) reptile, amphibian and fish dispersal rates (10, 5 and 50 km per decade) were already in `project.py`; birds carry the group range-shift verdict "fail" in `data/species/batch2_evidence/shift/`. No earlier asset is replaced (all new names start with `s2_`).

Run records and results: see sections 5 onward (filled in as the runs finish).


## 4. Curated animal ranges and the second-pass self-check

First pass: `ANIMALS` in `ctw/species/s3select.py` (continents and qualifiers, with a note on introduced populations and movements). Second pass (done after the first, by script plus reading): for every animal the GBIF record share by continent of the shortlist (`occ_continent_shares`, the top continents only) is compared with the curated continents; any share outside the mask of 3 percent or more is flagged and judged against what is known about the species. Result: 7 flagged, 6 confirmed as introduced or stocked populations that the mask rightly removes (blackbird in Australia and New Zealand, rhesus macaque in Florida, seven-spot ladybird and European hornet in North America, tegu in Florida, brown trout stocked in North America), and 1 correction (red-veined dropwing: 10 percent of records in Asia are native Arabia and Levant, mask widened to `AFRICA;ASIA(W)`). The remaining animals have all their shown record shares inside the curated continents. Limits [U]: the shares list only the top continents of the shortlist; the continent-level mask cannot separate native from introduced populations on the same continent (Perth rainbow lorikeets, stocked pike and golden perch inside their continents, Alaskan and Italian pike); the migration column reflects my knowledge of each species, not a data source.
